2. Data Quality & Preservation Audit

Preserving missingness semantics: UNKNOWN is uncertainty, never rejection or failure.

What
Audited duplicate rows, identifier collision, and field-level missingness distributions.
Why
Silently zeroing or deleting missing values destroys the simulation's clarification mechanism.
Result
100% ID uniqueness; distinct categorization of 'observed', 'not_asked', and 'declined'.
Impact
Protected the policy from invalid assumptions and premature candidate elimination.
The Data Preservation Axiom
"Missing values are meaningful in this challenge. Missing information is treated as UNCERTAINTY (needs_clarification) rather than rejection (infeasible)."

11. Exploration: Unlocking Edges with the Asking Budget

Maximizing candidate graph connectivity within the 12-unit daily constraint.

What
Engineered the Unlock Priority Metric for the 12-unit daily budget.
Why
Querying random members wastes budget; queries must unlock the highest number of edges.
Result
Hard bundle queries (3 units) for high-potential members unlock multiple edges at once.
Impact
Gave Policy V2.2 a principled information acquisition strategy.
The Clarification Allocation Formula
Value = complete_other * (0.5 + 0.5 * avg_fit) + 0.25 * max(0, potential - complete_other) + 0.05 / (1 + len(missing))

12. Exploitation: Bayesian Scoring & Scarcity Allocation

Selecting optimal, non-overlapping pairs from currently known information.

What
Formulated composite edge scoring combining feedback, soft fit, and scarcity.
Why
Greedy sorting by score alone strands isolated members and ignores historical feedback.
Result
Score = 0.55 * EmpiricalMean + 0.35 * SoftFit + 0.10 * UCB + ScarcityBonus.
Impact
Boosted mutual acceptances to 5.42 per 100 while maintaining 100% valid batches.

Demographics & DensityGeographic concentration is heavily skewed towards Zone A.Zone A represents 59.05% of the population, while Zone D represents only 4.4%.Members in Zone D face severe geographic supply scarcity. In sparse variants where zones expand to 12 clusters, candidate graphs fragment rapidly into disconnected components.
Demographics & AgeThe age distribution is centered around early 30s with moderate spread.Mean age is 33.65 (std: 7.6), with 26-35 accounting for over 50% of participants.Asymmetric age tolerance windows (e.g. ±3 to 10 years) filter out older participants (45+) who require younger partners or vice versa.
Information AsymmetrySmoking tolerance exhibits substantial missingness that acts as a hidden barrier.31.9% of partner smoking preferences are unobserved at Day 30.Because strict non-smokers reject smokers, unknown smoking status leaves thousands of otherwise viable pairs in an uncertain state.
Children & Family AlignmentStrong alignment exists between having children and accepting partner children, but unasked fields dominate.31.6% of existing children status is unobserved.Clarifying children constraints is one of the highest leverage operations to unlock high-probability long-term compatibility.
Soft Match DriverRelationship goal is the single most decisive compatibility attribute.Simulator ground truth assigns a +0.70 first-date weight and +0.40 second-date weight to matching relationship goals, yet 60.35% are unobserved.Matching pairs with opposing relationship goals (long_term vs exploring) leads to catastrophic attrition down the MSMI conversion funnel.
